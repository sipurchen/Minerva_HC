# Minerva HC Thinking

Use a small set of transferable cognitive tools to improve the user's actual work product. The Skill is inspired by Minerva University's public descriptions of Habits of Mind and Foundational Concepts; it is not an official or complete Minerva curriculum.

## Stand-down gate

Do not add a visible framework when the request is already narrow, factual, or mechanical. Apply ordinary craft and answer directly.

Use this Skill when at least one condition holds:

- The real problem, constraints, evidence, or stakeholders are unclear.
- The user wants a curriculum, decision, strategy, critique, or transfer exercise.
- A claim needs a plausibility, inference, or source-quality check.
- A draft needs behavioral feedback rather than general praise.
- A technique must work in more than one domain.

## Workflow

1. **Frame the performance.** State what the user must decide, create, explain, or demonstrate. Separate verified facts, assumptions, and unknowns.
2. **Select 2–4 habits.** Choose only habits that will change the output. Prefer complementary habits across the four competency families: critical thinking, creative thinking, effective communication, and effective interaction.
3. **Apply them to artifacts.** Produce checkable work such as an evidence ledger, competing inferences, constraint table, prototype comparison, audience revision, or stakeholder map. Give concise rationale, not hidden chain-of-thought.
4. **Transfer once.** Reapply at least one selected habit to a meaningfully different example or domain when the task is educational or evaluative.
5. **Assess behavior.** Score only observable application using the adapted 1–5 rubric. Cite the evidence for the score and name the next practice step.

## Core habit menu

- **Critical thinking:** plausibility estimation, induction with competing explanations, evidence/source quality, decision costs and benefits.
- **Creative thinking:** model choice, constraint satisfaction, problem decomposition, iterative design.
- **Effective communication:** precise composition, audience fit, multimodal design.
- **Effective interaction:** stakeholder interests, negotiation structure, team differences, ethical and social effects.

Read [references/hc-framework.md](references/hc-framework.md) when selecting detailed habits, designing a course or rubric, building a transfer task, or verifying the public-source boundary.

## Output contract

Use the smallest useful form:

- **Task frame:** one or two sentences.
- **Selected habits:** tag, purpose, and the artifact each habit will change.
- **Applied result:** the revised decision, design, lesson, critique, or plan.
- **Transfer check:** one new context, when relevant.
- **Rubric evidence:** level 1–5 plus the observed behavior, when assessment is requested.
- **Unknowns:** claims that remain unverified.

## Boundaries

- Do not claim that the habit list is Minerva's current or complete catalog.
- Do not reproduce or invent private Minerva course materials or proprietary rubrics.
- Do not use an HC label as decoration; omit any habit that does not change the result.
- Do not invent evidence or real-world cases. Label hypothetical examples.
- Preserve user authority and existing action permissions. Analysis does not authorize side effects.
- For medical, legal, financial, security, or other high-stakes decisions, verify current sources and state professional-review limits.
